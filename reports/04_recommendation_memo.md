# Recommendation Memo

**To:** Head of Sales, PT Segar Nusantara
**Cc:** Area Sales Managers; Finance (credit control); Sales Ops analytics
**Date:** 1 October 2026
**Re:** Replace the "longest-silent first" follow-up list with the Churn Early-Warning visit list

---

### The ask

Approve a **three-month pilot (October to December 2026)** in which the 200 monthly retention visits follow the Churn Early-Warning list instead of the current recency report, with 10% of flagged outlets held back as a control group.

### Why

Every month, retail partners that go quiet take **about Rp 10.4 bn of annual revenue** with them. Our follow-up today chases whoever has been silent longest. That finds churners, but mostly small ones: in a back-test over May to July 2026 it pointed reps at **44%** of the revenue we were about to lose.

The new list ranks outlets by **expected revenue lost** (chance of going quiet × what they buy from us). In the same back-test it pointed reps at **69%** of revenue-at-risk with the same 200 visits, while keeping a hit rate close to today's (44% vs 48% of listed outlets actually churned).

| With 200 visits a month | Today's method | New list |
|---|---:|---:|
| Revenue-at-risk on the list | 44% | **69%** |
| Annual revenue of churning outlets put in front of reps, per month | ≈ Rp 5.6 bn | **≈ Rp 8.7 bn** |
| Listed outlets that really churned (hit rate) | 48% | 44% |
| Churning warungs reached | 53% | 34% |

### What it could be worth

If a retention visit saves **one in four** of the at-risk outlets it reaches (an assumption the pilot will measure), the extra Rp 3.1 bn of at-risk revenue reached each month is worth **≈ Rp 0.8 bn of annual revenue retained per monthly cycle**, or roughly **Rp 9 bn of annualised revenue** over a year of cycles. At a 1-in-10 save rate it is still ≈ Rp 3.7 bn. The cost is the analyst time to run it monthly; the visits are already budgeted.

### What changes for the field team

- On the 1st of each month, each Area Sales Manager gets their outlets from the list, each with **up to three reasons and a suggested action** (e.g. *"Pays 23 days late on average → agree a payment plan with Finance before pushing new stock"*).
- 160 slots go to the biggest expected losses; **40 are reserved for warungs**, so small partners are not dropped.
- Reps can skip an outlet they know is fine, with a one-line reason. Those skips are reviewed monthly.

### Two things that need action now, outside the model

1. **Jawa Timur is in a new regime.** Churn there has been above its statistical control limits every month since January 2026 (20.8% vs 9.0% nationally), coinciding with a rival distributor's entry. 80 of October's 200 visits are in Jawa Timur. Visits alone will not fix a competitive problem. **Recommend a regional pricing and availability review.**
2. **Late payment is the strongest early signal.** Outlets paying more than 14 days late churned at 47% vs 7%. **Recommend Finance and Sales agree a joint playbook** (payment plan before stock push) rather than credit holds that accelerate the exit.

### Risks and how we manage them

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Visits may not actually save outlets (we only know they *correlate*) | 10% randomised holdout; decide on scale-up in January using measured save rate |
| List quality degrades as the market shifts | Monthly drift check and back-test once outcomes land (60-day lag); retrain quarterly |
| Reps distrust a "black box" | Every outlet shows plain-language reasons; the dashboard explains the method |
| Small outlets neglected | Reserved warung lane; segment coverage reported monthly |
